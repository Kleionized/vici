
VICI Questionnaire Changes
Complete change log + final production copy
Scope: questionnaire and questionnaire-adjacent transition screens only. This document intentionally excludes personalized result screens, pattern scoring, result visualizations, VICI rating logic, projections, reminder personalization, and all post-questionnaire profile customization.
INCLUDED
Screens 03–24: wording, options, deletions, added questions, branching required to administer the questionnaire, field names, and transition copy.
EXCLUDED
Screens 25 onward: personalized setup/driver/window results, interventions, plan intensity, rating, projections, “what you get back,” reminders, etc.
1. Global questionnaire wording rules
Say the thing directly. Use “watch porn,” “quit,” “cut down,” “relapse,” “in bed,” “scrolling,” etc. Avoid euphemisms such as “when it happens,” “start looking,” or “your porn use” when a simpler phrase exists.
Ask about remembered behaviour. Prefer questions a user can answer from a concrete memory over abstract self-ratings. Example: “How long do you usually make it before watching again?” is better than “How often do you still end up watching?”
Do not use “relapse” too early. Only use relapse language after the user has said they have tried to quit. A user who has never attempted abstinence has no relapse history to report.
No dramatic identity language. Remove options such as “I can’t remember a time when it wasn’t an issue.” They are emotionally loaded and do not add useful precision.
Keep options conversational. Use phrases that sound like things a person would naturally say: “Nothing obvious,” “Now and then,” “I’m not sure yet,” “Keep it, just without porn.”
Do not sneak in a diagnosis. Question labels and options should describe behaviour, context, and goals. Avoid “severity,” “self-control problem,” “addiction type,” or other clinical/moral framing.
Keep porn and masturbation separate. The questionnaire must preserve a user’s stated masturbation goal. “Keep it, just without porn” must not be silently converted into abstinence.
Use conditional questions when they are genuinely relevant. Do not ask everyone about relapse history, loneliness, or time alone if earlier answers make those questions irrelevant.
Phrases to remove from questionnaire copy
“when it happens”
“start looking”
“wanted to change your porn use”
“I can’t remember a time when it wasn’t an issue”
“Almost every time / More often than not / About half the time / Not very often” as a control scale
“There isn’t one clear thing”
“Self-control” as an affected-area label
“Your target” as a heading
“Analysing your behaviour,” “identifying your triggers,” “generating your personalised recovery plan,” or other fake-AI loading language
