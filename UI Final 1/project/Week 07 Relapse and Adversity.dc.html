Some days are just harder
A rough day does not require a catastrophe. You may have slept badly, had an irritating day at work, watched plans fall through, and spent too long online. Stop trying to rescue the whole day
A rough day often creates another problem: you try to repair everything before bed. Suddenly you need to exercise, catch up on work, fix your mood, cook properly, answer every message, and restore the whole routine. That is too much to ask from a day already going badly. Eat, put the phone away, and get to sleep without making the evening worse.

Use the bad-day minimum you chose
Last week you chose one or two things you would still do on a bad day. Use them now. That may mean no second relapse, phone outside the room, one proper meal, sleep, going outside once, or calling somebody. Do not add ten more goals because the day has gone badly.
Use it.

A bad mood is not the moment to browse your strongest triggers and prove your discipline survived. Stay near people, work in the library, keep the device visible, use Vici's Urge Surfing shortcut if you need it, or go to bed without the phone. A rough day calls for fewer opportunities to drift toward porn, not a test of how much temptation you can handle.
