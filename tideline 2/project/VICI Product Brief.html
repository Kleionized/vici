Product brief · July 2026

VICI

Veni, vidi, vici — “I came, I saw, I conquered.” A recovery app for quitting porn that treats the habit as a campaign to be won once, not a streak to be defended forever.

Product description

VICI is a private, science-based companion for men and women breaking a compulsive pornography habit. Where most quit-apps are built around a streak counter — a number that grows until one bad night erases it — VICI is built around conquest: a twelve-week guided campaign that rewires the habit loop underneath the urge, so that ground you take stays taken. A slip in VICI is a data point, never a reset to zero.

The product rests on three mechanics. First, a structured journey — ten themed “worlds” from sea to summit, each a set of short interactive lessons (2–6 minutes) drawn from CBT and urge-surfing practice: why the loop exists, how cravings crest and pass, how to dismantle triggers, and what to build in the habit's place. Second, an in-the-moment toolkit — a one-tap urge button that lives in the center of the navigation, always reachable. It asks two fast questions (where are you, how strong is it) and routes to a path sized to the moment: a quiet reframe for a flicker, or an immediate, location-specific escape plan plus a three-minute breathing wave when the pull is at its worst. Third, a reflective record — daily mood check-ins on a five-tone scale, urge logs with triggers and outcomes, a journal, and weekly insight reports that surface patterns (late nights, boredom, stress) the user can act on the following week.

The emotional register is deliberately unlike the category: no shame, no red warning screens, no gamified guilt. The interface is a calm paper world — warm parchment, an engraved serif voice, faceted monochrome landscapes — closer to a well-set book than a dashboard. Progress is honored with keepsakes (earned medallions for real actions: riding out ten urges, writing an honest entry, letting someone in) and a letter from day-zero you, delivered three days in. The promise on the wordmark is the promise of the product: one day you close the app for good, and it says vici.

  • Platform: iOS-first mobile app (390 × 844 design canvas)
  • Model: free onboarding + core urge tool; Plus subscription ($39.99/yr, or $99.99/yr with coach) unlocks the full curriculum, insights, and keepsakes
  • Privacy: everything on-device by default; Face ID app lock; no social feed
  • Voice: steady, plain, quietly Roman — “Day XXIV,” “The tide is rising,” “Don't fail twice”

Screen overview — “Today” (home)

The home screen is the app's north star; every other surface borrows its grammar.

The screen sits on a warm parchment ground. At the very top, three quiet objects form the header: a small streak pill on the left (a flame glyph and the day count), the centered eyebrow “DAY XXIV” — Gill Sans capitals, wide-tracked, underlined by a short 40-pixel ink rule — and a round avatar on the right. The Roman numeral is intentional: days are counted like a campaign, not a scoreboard.

Below the header comes the screen's one indulgence: a large grey quotation mark, set in Newsreader at sixty pixels and a fifth of full ink, floating above a centered serif maxim — a single editorial sentence (“Small steps. Steady days. That's how it changes.”). It reads as the day's epigraph rather than a motivational banner.

Then the day's work, in three sections, each introduced by the same section label: small caps, semibold, letterspaced, sitting outside its card with a muted meta note on the right.

1 · Next lesson

A solid near-black (#131313) card, the only dark object on the page — which is exactly why the eye lands on it. Inside: a faint inverted mountain-path scene, the eyebrow “NEXT LESSON” in translucent paper caps, the lesson title (“Riding out a craving”) in white serif, a duration note, and a white circular go-button with an ink arrow. Dark cards are reserved app-wide for the one thing that matters now — this is the day's summit.

2 · Today's steps

A white card holding a four-item checklist divided by hairlines — drink a glass of water, take a ten-minute walk, two minutes of slow breathing, log how you're feeling. Each row is a full-width tap target with a 28-pixel circle that fills solid ink and gains a white check when done; the label strikes through and fades. The section label's meta reads “2 of 4.” No progress bar, no confetti — the quiet arithmetic is the reward.

3 · Your week

A seven-day strip, one letter per day. Logged days are solid discs colored from the five-step mood ramp — pale warm grey (low) deepening to near-black (radiant) — the same ramp the check-in slider, the emotion wheel, and the analytics all share, so a glance at the row is a glance at the week's weather. Today is a tappable ring that cycles through the tones; future days are faint plus-marks. Nothing here is a number.

Navigation

A flat, parchment-colored tab bar closes the screen: Today, Journey, then a raised dark circle bearing a white wave — the urge button, deliberately the largest and darkest target on screen so it can be found mid-crisis without reading — then Log and You. Thin stroke-line icons, Gill Sans labels, no floating glass, no shadows.

The design argument of the screen: one dark thing to do next, a short list of small steps, and a week you can read at a glance — with the way out of an urge always one thumb-reach away.
