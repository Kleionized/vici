VICI — Revised Onboarding Flow
Login first, profile details early, plain questions, and a stronger finish that makes the value of changing feel real.
What changed in this revision
Login is back at the start. There is no second “save your progress” account screen near the paywall.
First name and age come immediately after login. Gender follows as an optional profile field for future personalisation.
Removed the “Start Today” readiness question and the “Daily Plan” preference question. Neither adds enough information to justify another screen.
Removed the entire “How Much Support You Want” section. VICI should have a strong default daily experience rather than asking the user to design the product during onboarding.
The curriculum map is back to three screens: Weeks I–IV, V–VIII, and IX–XII. This is purely for spacing and readability.
Restored the Week XII letter after the curriculum map. It is a future-self note about continuing after hard nights or slips, not a promise that the next twelve weeks will be perfect.
Questions still follow one rule: ask one concrete thing at a time, with answers that all respond to the same question.
The flow below has 43 screens. The first half stays factual and low-pressure. The later screens become more motivating only after VICI has enough information to make that motivation specific to the user.
Tone through the flow
Keep the assessment plain. Do not try to motivate the user while asking factual questions. From the personalised results onward, let the emotional weight rise: show the cost of staying the same, then give the user a believable way forward. Use their own answers wherever possible. The paywall should feel like continuing a plan they already want, not like a scare tactic.

38 — A Letter From Week XII
[Name] -
I'm writing this at the end of week XII.
I remember where you are right now. You want this to be the time it finally changes, but part of you is already wondering how long you'll last. I was thinking the same thing.
So here is the part I wish you knew: you do not need twelve perfect weeks. You need to stop turning one bad night into a reason to quit.
There were nights when I wanted to watch badly. There were nights when I spent twenty minutes arguing with myself. Some days the only thing I did right was get through the evening. That still counted.
And there were bad days. What changed was what happened after them. I stopped saying, 'I've ruined it now, so what is the point?' I came back the next morning. I logged what happened. I carried on.
At the start, porn still felt like the quickest way to get relief when I was bored, stressed, lonely, tired, or just fed up. That did not disappear in a week. But every time I waited it out, left the room, put the phone down, or used the SOS instead, I learned something simple: the urge ends whether you obey it or not.
After a while, that changes you. You stop being so scared of an urge because you have seen enough of them come and go. You stop treating every difficult night like an emergency. You start trusting yourself again.
The biggest difference is not a streak. It is waking up without that same feeling that you gave another night away. It is having more time and attention for the things you said you wanted back. It is making a promise to yourself and, more often than before, actually keeping it.
I am not going to tell you everything is fixed here. It is not. I still have bad days. I still get urges sometimes. The difference is that they are not running the whole show anymore.
If you are reading this on day 0, do not try to get to week XII tonight. Get through tonight. Tomorrow, come back. If you have a bad day, come back anyway. If you slip, do not disappear for a week because you are embarrassed. Come back the next morning.
That is how you make it out: you keep coming back long enough for things to change.
You can do this. Not because it will suddenly become easy, but because hard nights end. Urges end. Bad days end. And none of them have to be the day you give up.
Do not quit on yourself halfway through.
I'll see you at week XII.
- [Name], Week XII

Writing Rules for the Final UI
Ask one concrete thing per screen. If the answers describe different kinds of information, split the question or remove it.
Keep answer sets parallel. A frequency question gets frequency answers; a “what happened first?” question gets events.
Do not ask readiness questions such as “Are you ready to start?” The user has already chosen to go through onboarding.
Do not ask the user to design VICI’s daily experience. Give them a clear default and let settings change it later.
Prefer ordinary actions and situations over abstractions. “I get into bed with my phone” is better than “automatic pattern.”
Use the user’s name at high-value moments only. Two or three uses across onboarding is enough.
Personalisation must change something real: risk time changes reminders; location and trigger change SOS actions; goal changes the vow and relevant lesson framing.
If there is no strong signal in the answers, say less. Do not manufacture a root cause or an exact risk window.
Do not use exact times, percentages, or projections that the collected data cannot support.
End onboarding decisively. The user should reach the paywall wanting to continue the plan, not merely wanting to escape the projection. After the paywall, the next screen is the real product.
