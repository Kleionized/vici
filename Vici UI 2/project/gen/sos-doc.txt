
Vici SOS pages and decision tree
SOS starts with a 1–5 urge rating, then asks where the person is, what is feeding the urge and how they feel. It uses those answers and the local time to suggest three concrete actions. The person picks one and can see more choices. Afterward, they can rate the urge again or leave.
The action library has 12 situations with ten choices each. Some activities appear in more than one situation because the instructions differ. The app filters the choices before showing them and keeps one action active at a time. The screen plan uses the supplied location and chip layouts, with shorter headings where they make the question clearer.
The screen sequence
Urge rating → Location → What is feeding it → Feelings → Choose an action → Do the action → Optional check.
Only the first four screens are questions before the recommendations. Show four progress marks during those questions. Keep the back arrow and close control in the same position as the supplied screens. Closing leaves SOS without a confirmation question. Keep answers when the user goes back.
Screen 1 Rate the urge
Heading: “How strong is the urge right now?”
Five large choices: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5. Put “Mild” beneath 1 and “Very strong” beneath 5. The selected number uses the same light fill and dark text as the selected answers in the references. Start with no number selected. Button: “Continue”. Small secondary action: “Give me something now”.
Use five points for the first version. It is quick to tap and easy to repeat afterward. Do not switch to ten points on the final screen. The number describes how strong the urge feels to the person; it is not a diagnosis or a prediction of what they will do.
For 4 or 5, the first recommendations should take one to three minutes and have few steps. A lower score can put a five-minute activity first. The user can still choose a longer task. Keep the wording the same at every score; a 5 does not change the screen into an alarm or a lecture.
“Give me something now” opens two choices without requiring the remaining questions: “Put the phone down for two minutes” and “Look at three things near you for one minute”. The rating can be left unanswered when this shortcut is used.
Screen 2 Choose the location
Heading: “Where are you right now?”
Use the five large rows from the reference:
Somewhere private
In bed
A public space
At work or school
Out and about
Button: “Continue”. A small “Skip” link uses options that do not depend on knowing the location. Do not preselect Somewhere private. In bed is its own answer even though it is usually private. A public space means somewhere the user is staying, such as a library; Out and about means they are away from home and moving between places. Both answers use the same limits until the user chooses a more specific action.
For In bed, show one small extra choice on this page: “Are you trying to sleep?” Answers: “Yes”, “No, I’m staying up”, “I just woke up”, “Not sure”. This follows the person’s plans, including a daytime sleep after a night shift. If they skip it, start with quiet choices that can be done where they are.
Somewhere private does not establish that the person is at home or has a kitchen. A household card needs a simple “I’m at home” check before starting if that is still unknown. At work or school puts seated actions first and shows walking or washing options only when they have a suitable break.

Subtitle: “Select all that apply.”
Use chips in the layout from the reference:

Doomscrolling
A thought I keep returning to
Phone in bed
My usual routine
Can’t sleep
An argument
Being alone
Putting something off
I don’t know
Button: “Continue”. Use a tick and the light fill for selected chips. “I don’t know” clears other answers, and choosing another chip clears “I don’t know”. A small Skip link has the same effect as leaving the cause unknown. Longer chip labels may use two lines; keep their text readable instead of shrinking the font to fit.
These choices describe what the person recognises in the moment. Something online does not tell the app that porn is still open. A recurring thought does not mean the person needs to stop having the thought. The action cards can ask them to close a feed or start another activity without making either claim.

Turned on

Button: “Show my options”. Keep the selected style from the reference. “I don’t know” is exclusive, as on the previous page. Turned on can be selected beside another feeling. Leave every chip unselected at first.
The question is direct. It does not imply that a hidden feeling must explain the urge. Hunger is added because it can make a food action practical; selecting Tired alone does not make a snack the right choice.
Screen 5 Pick an action
Heading: “Pick one thing to do now.”
Show three cards with a title, time and one concrete first step. A short line above them can explain the fit: “It’s late and you want to sleep. These can be done quietly.” Use only information the person supplied. If time was the only extra context, say “It’s late. Here are a few quiet options.”
Show the steps before starting, either inside the expanded card or on a short detail view. Button on the chosen card: “Start this”. Secondary: “More options”. Keep “Change options” small but visible. It opens 2, 5 or 15 minutes, plus Seated only, Quiet and Stay indoors. Default to five minutes and a free choice. Filters can be changed without repeating the four questions.
At least one first card should need no item and no movement. Confirm known resources before calling a card ready. A single clearly labelled preview, such as “Needs a book”, can occupy one of the three places; tapping it checks that item before starting. The AI ranks ready cards. The app can insert the preview from the library when it adds useful variety.
“More options” shows suitable cards from the selected situation, then alternatives from another relevant situation. Ten cards belong to each situation; the app does not have to display ten when some cannot be done there. Avoid filling the first three places with three versions of putting down the phone, three walks or three reading tasks.
Outdoor choices are available under “Show outdoor options” when the person wants them. Before an errand starts, confirm that they know a nearby open shop, can make the whole trip within their chosen time, and want to buy one item. Keep a free alternative beside it. The app uses the shop and route the person already knows; it does not need a map page or a location permission prompt.
Screen 6 Do the action
Show the chosen title and its exact steps. For a timed action, the button can say “Start 3 minutes”. For a counted action, use “Start”. Once started, show “You can put the phone down now.” A timer continues when the screen locks. Sound is off unless the user chooses it.
One action stays active. Do not add a journal prompt, a second exercise or a message to send. A shop errand includes the walk there, buying one item and returning as one activity. A person can stop early or choose another card. The timer measures the chosen time; it does not promise the urge will be gone afterward.
Buttons when they return: “I did it” and “Choose something else”. They can leave without checking in. An errand does not require phone interaction while walking.
Screen 7 Optional check
Heading: “How strong is the urge now?”
Use the same 1–5 choices with the same anchors. Add a separate “It’s gone” option, stored as 0 after the action. Do not place a default rating on this page. Buttons: “Done” and “Try another option”. A small “Skip” link finishes the session.
After a rating, show a plain response. For a lower score: “It feels less strong now.” For the same score: “It feels about the same. You can choose another action or finish here.” For a higher score: “It feels stronger. Pick another action if you want to.” If no starting rating exists, acknowledge only the new answer. Avoid calculating a change from a missing value.
An unchanged score does not undo a completed task. Trying another option returns to the cards with the answers intact and puts a different activity first. There is no repeated questionnaire and no required score before the person can finish.
Choosing the three cards
Use the rules below in order. Score and time affect the length and effort of the first choices. They do not override the location or make unavailable things available.
1. A confirmed sleep goal selects B. Can’t sleep also suggests a sleep goal, unless the person explicitly said they intend to stay up. A hungry person can get one suitable ready snack from I alongside bedtime choices.
2. Just woke up selects C when the person wants to get up. At work, school or in public, the location still restricts which C cards can be used.
3. At work or school and A public space start with K. Out and about also starts with K until the user chooses a suitable outdoor option. Somewhere private keeps household items unconfirmed.
4. Match the selected trigger and feelings to the library. Something online or Doomscrolling selects A; My usual routine or Bored selects D; Stressed or anxious selects E; Being alone or Lonely selects F; An argument or Angry selects G; Putting something off selects H; Tired or Hungry selects I; Turned on or a recurring thought selects J; Low or Restless selects L.
5. When the trigger and feeling point to different situations, use one card addressing the trigger, one addressing the feeling, and one easy general option, where practical. A sleep goal or restricted location can supply all three instead. Do not use the order of chip taps as evidence of what matters most.
6. For 4 or 5, put an eligible one-to-three-minute action first. For 3, put an action taking two to five minutes first. For 1 or 2, rank by the situation and the selected time. These are interface choices to test, not clinical cutoffs.
7. Apply the time budget, indoor, quiet, seated, item and setting checks. Unknown resources get a small check on the selected card; they are not silently treated as present. For a task the person is avoiding, offer a small selector for Document, Study, Email, Dishes, Tidying, Laundry, Bill or letter, or Other. Use only matching task cards.
8. Remove duplicate activity families. If too few actions are ready, use J01, J02 or L01 as appropriate. Show fewer than three if only two different activities fit. The person picks one.
Phone in bed alone does not mean the person wants sleep. Use the answer on the location page. An argument plus Lonely might produce a short pause away from the message thread, an in-person option if someone is there, and a quiet independent task. If no one is there, keep the independent options.
For 4 or 5, do not lead with a shop trip, a library visit or a task needing several supplies. Those can still appear under More options once they fit the person’s choices. If all questions are skipped, use two short equipment-free options and let them choose whether to answer more.
Time rules
Read the device’s local time when the cards appear, and again after an action. Show a small line such as “12:18 am”. The person can correct it. Their sleep answer takes priority over the clock. Clock bands are starting defaults for the interface, not statements about when urges occur.

Midnight does not automatically mean bed, and morning does not automatically mean getting up. A person sleeping at 11 am after a shift gets B. A person at work at 1 am gets K. At night, do not offer a trip just because a shop might be open.
Examples of the complete route
1. 12:20 am, rating 5, In bed, wants sleep, Phone in bed, Turned on and Tired. First choices: B01 move the phone, B05 straighten the pillow and cover, B09 rest with the screen down. These need no new purchase or other person. More options can include a confirmed book or a brief face wash.
2. 8:10 am, rating 3, In bed, Just woke up, My usual routine and Bored. First choices: C01 get dressed, C05 pack three things and J01 put the phone down, subject to the clothes and bag checks. A breakfast errand is a longer option if wanted and practical.
3. 3 pm, rating 2, Somewhere private, My usual routine and Bored. Start with D choices. Once home and items are confirmed, offer a five-minute walk, clearing one small surface or folding five clothes. A shop card appears if outdoor options are chosen and the trip fits.
4. 2 pm, rating 4, At work or school, Doomscrolling, Stressed or anxious. First choices: K01 put the phone away, K06 look at three nearby things, and K05 start two minutes of the next task if a task is available. A corridor break requires a suitable break.
5. 11:50 pm, rating 4, Somewhere private, Being alone, Lonely and Low. Use a short screen pause, two pages of a book if available, or one small household action after confirming home. An in-person card appears only if someone is actually there and awake.
6. 6:30 pm, rating 3, Out and about, Something online, Restless. Start with putting the phone away or looking at three nearby things from a seat. If outdoor options are chosen, offer a familiar five-minute route or a short one-item shop errand after checking time and opening hours.
7. 1 am, rating 3, At work or school, My usual routine, Bored. Use K with the shift’s constraints. Do not route to bed because of the clock.
8. 11 am, rating 4, In bed after a night shift, wants sleep, Can’t sleep and Tired. Use B. Do not suggest getting dressed for the day or visiting a shop.
AI behaviour
The app applies the decision tree and practical checks first. The AI receives the relevant answers, local time or broad time band, and the ready action IDs. It returns approved IDs plus at most one short sentence. Titles and steps come from the library exactly as written. It does not invent new app tools, places, opening hours, explanations of motives or treatment claims.
If the AI is slow or unavailable, show the same suitable choices from the decision tree. Do not make the person wait for a chat response. A later response must not reorder cards after selection has begun. Shortcuts and timers should work without AI.
The ACT element is light: notice a thought or feeling, then start one chosen action even if it is still there. Use “You can start while the urge is still there” sparingly. Do not make an acceptance exercise a compulsory step before every task. Do not label ordinary arousal or a sexual thought as a lapse.
Keep session answers temporary unless the existing app already has a relevant user-controlled way to save them. The SOS flow needs no written description. Put sources in the separate research notes, with none on the user-facing screens.
Visual details to carry forward
Use the reference screens’ dark background, light selected states, rounded rows, chip spacing and bottom Continue button. Keep the heading clear of the top controls. The urge scale needs five equal tap areas that fit without horizontal scrolling. On chip pages, allow the longest answer to wrap rather than changing type size between chips.
The location page can keep the doorway illustration. The other illustrations are optional; when a small screen or larger text makes the page scroll, shorten or remove the illustration before compressing answer buttons. Keep enough space for the bottom button and the device’s safe area. At large text sizes, scroll the answers above the button. Use ticks as well as colour for multi select.
Use the weather illustration as decoration only. It should not imply that the AI has identified a hidden emotional state. The phone illustration does not imply a locking feature. The action page needs the steps and timer more than a large illustration.
Checks before the app is updated
Walk through the eight examples above, including skipped questions, several selected feelings and a session crossing midnight. Check that rating 5 still leads to a choice, that sleep overrides the clock, and that unavailable items are never shown as confirmed.
Check all 120 action IDs, ten cards per situation, the time estimates and the task matching. Test seated and quiet filters, unavailable AI, a closed shop, a two-minute budget and an action stopped early. Check the selected and unselected controls at normal and large text sizes. Measure how long it takes a first-time user to choose an action; revise the number of questions if people abandon the flow.
The exact decision tree and individual errands have not been clinically tested. Test whether people understand the questions, can start a practical action and can leave SOS without getting stuck in repeat timers.
Action library
Each situation has ten choices. The user picks one. Resource labels and completion lines below are for the build; the app shows only the checks needed for the chosen card.
A Something online or doomscrolling
Use when: User selects Something online or Doomscrolling. Choose a concrete way to close the feed and do something away from it.
Opening copy: “Close what you were looking at. Pick one thing to do next.”
A01 Put the device down for three minutes
Close the content and lock the screen. Put the device face down on a surface just beyond easy reach. Leave it there for three minutes. You can remain seated.
About 3 min. Fits: Can be seated; Can be quiet.
Done: The content is closed and the device stayed down for three minutes.
A02 Shut the laptop and leave the chair
Close the content, shut the laptop lid, and move to a different chair or room for three minutes. Leave the laptop where it is.
About 3 min. Fits: Laptop can be left; Indoor movement suitable; Can be quiet.
Done: You spent three minutes away from the laptop.
A03 Walk indoors for five minutes
Close the content. Put the phone in your pocket or on a shelf and walk between two clear points indoors for five minutes at your usual pace.
About 5 min. Fits: Indoor movement suitable; Can be quiet.
Done: You walked for five minutes without reopening the content.
A04 Wash one mug
Close the content and leave the device outside the kitchen. Wash, rinse and dry one mug, then put it away.
About 2–3 min. Fits: At home; Accessible kitchen; Water or sink available; Indoor movement suitable; Can be quiet.
Done: One mug is washed and put away.
A05 Read two pages
Close the content and put the device down. Open a physical book at your bookmark and read the next two pages.
About 3–5 min. Fits: Physical book; Can be seated; Can be quiet.
Done: You read two pages with the content closed.
A06 Sit with someone at home
Close the content. Ask an awake person you trust, “Mind if I sit here for a few minutes?” Stay in the shared room for five minutes if they agree.
About 5 min. Fits: At home; Awake willing person at home; Suitable shared place or people; Indoor movement suitable.
Done: You spent five minutes in their company.
A07 Stay seated and look around
Close what you were looking at and turn the screen face down. For one minute, look at three ordinary objects near you and notice their colours and shapes.
About 1 min. Fits: Can be seated; Can be quiet.
Done: You spent one minute looking at the room with the content closed.
A08 Fold three clean items
Close the feed and put the phone down. Fold three clean clothes or towels and put them together on a chair or shelf. Stop after three.
About 3 min. Fits: At home; Clean clothes; Can be seated; Can be quiet.
Done: Three clean items are folded.
A09 Put the phone inside your bag for three minutes
Close what you were looking at and lock the screen. Put the phone inside your bag, close the bag and leave it there for three minutes.
About 3 min. Fits: Bag and belongings; Can be seated; Can be quiet.
Done: The phone stayed in the closed bag for three minutes.
A10 Put three things ready by the door
Close the feed and put the phone down. Put your keys, bag and the item you need for your next planned outing beside the door. Use the three things you actually need, then stop.
About 3 min. Fits: At home; Bag and belongings; Intends to stay awake; Can be quiet.
Done: Three things are ready for your next planned outing.
B In bed and trying to sleep
Use when: User confirms they want sleep, including sleep after a shift. Quiet short actions take priority.
Opening copy: “Choose something quiet you can do before trying to sleep again.”
B01 Move the phone away from the pillow
Put the phone on a stable surface across the room, or as far from the pillow as you can comfortably reach. Leave your usual alarm set. Settle back into bed without taking the phone with you.
About 1–2 min. Fits: Wants sleep; In bed; Can be seated; Can be quiet.
Done: The phone is away from the pillow and you have returned to bed.
B02 Read two pages in dim light
Put the phone down. Read two pages of a familiar physical book in comfortable dim light, then put the book aside when you are ready to try sleeping.
About 3–5 min. Fits: Wants sleep; Physical book; Can be seated; Can be quiet.
Done: You read two pages without using a feed.
B03 Wash your face and return to bed
Leave the phone behind. Wash your face with ordinary comfortable-temperature water, dry it, and return to bed.
About 2 min. Fits: Wants sleep; At home; Water or sink available; Indoor movement suitable; Can be quiet.
Done: You washed your face and returned without the phone.
B04 Take a few sips at the kitchen table
If you are thirsty, leave the phone in the bedroom, pour a small glass of water and sit at the table for two minutes. Return to bed when ready.
About 2–3 min. Fits: Wants sleep; At home; Accessible kitchen; Water or sink available; Wants water; Indoor movement suitable; Can be quiet.
Done: You had water and two quiet minutes away from the screen.
B05 Straighten the pillow and cover
Put the phone down. Adjust the pillow and sheet or duvet until you are comfortable, then lie down for two minutes with the screen closed. You can do this from bed.
About 2 min. Fits: Wants sleep; In bed; Can be seated; Can be quiet.
Done: You spent two minutes settled in bed with the screen closed.
B06 Fold three items quietly
If clean clothes are within reach, put the phone down and fold three items. Put them together on a chair or shelf, then return to bed.
About 2–3 min. Fits: Wants sleep; At home; Clean clothes; Can be seated; Can be quiet.
Done: Three items are folded.
B07 Sit in a different chair briefly
Leave the phone down and move to a comfortable chair in another part of the room or home. Sit in low light for three minutes.
About 3 min. Fits: Wants sleep; At home; Indoor movement suitable; Can be quiet.
Done: You sat away from the screen for three minutes.
B08 Put out clothes for when you get up
Leave the phone down and put the clothes you plan to wear after sleeping on a chair. Stop after this one preparation and return to bed.
About 2 min. Fits: Wants sleep; At home; Clean clothes; Can be quiet.
Done: Your clothes are ready for when you get up.
B09 Rest for one minute with the screen down
Put the phone face down. Notice where the mattress or chair supports you for one minute, breathing normally. If focusing on your body feels uncomfortable, look at one object in the room instead.
About 1 min. Fits: Wants sleep; Can be seated; Can be quiet.
Done: You spent one minute with the screen down.
B10 Close your eyes for three minutes
Put the phone down, make yourself comfortable and close your eyes for three minutes. If a thought about watching comes up, notice it and stay where you are.
About 3 min. Fits: Wants sleep; Rest is suitable; Can be seated; Can be quiet.
Done: You rested for three minutes without opening the content.
C Just woke up and getting up
Use when: User selects Just woke up and wants to get up. Clock time alone does not select this bank.
Opening copy: “Pick the first thing you want to do after getting up.”
C01 Get dressed before opening anything else
Put the phone down and change out of your sleep clothes into the clothes you plan to wear today. Keep the screen closed until you are dressed.
About 3–5 min. Fits: Wants to get up; At home; Clean clothes; Can be quiet.
Done: You are dressed for the day.
C02 Brush your teeth without the phone
Leave the phone where it is, go to the sink and brush your teeth for your usual two minutes. Continue with your usual routine after getting up.
About 2–3 min. Fits: Wants to get up; At home; Water or sink available; Indoor movement suitable; Toothbrush and usual supplies; Can be quiet.
Done: You brushed your teeth without the phone.
C03 Eat breakfast at the table
If you are hungry, make a breakfast you normally eat, such as toast or cereal. Sit at a table to eat it with the phone put down.
About 5–10 min. Fits: Wants to get up; At home; Accessible kitchen; Hungry and suitable food available.
Done: You prepared and ate breakfast away from the feed.
C04 Make the bed
Leave the phone on a surface away from the bed. Straighten the sheet or duvet and put the pillows back. Leave the bedroom when you finish if you can.
About 2–3 min. Fits: Wants to get up; At home; Indoor movement suitable; Can be quiet.
Done: The bed is made.
C05 Pack three things for the day
Put your keys, wallet or travel card, and any item you need for work or study into your bag. Use your own equivalents; stop after the bag is ready.
About 3 min. Fits: Wants to get up; Bag and belongings; Can be seated; Can be quiet.
Done: The bag is ready with the three things you need.
C06 Walk a short familiar route
Get dressed and take a familiar five-minute route from your front door and back at your usual pace. Keep the phone locked while moving.
About 5–8 min. Fits: Wants to get up; Suitable familiar outdoor route; Suitable daytime or evening.
Done: You completed the short route and returned.
C07 Buy one breakfast item
Walk to the nearest convenience store or supermarket you know is open and within a short familiar trip. Buy one item you actually want for breakfast, then come home. Choose a free option if you do not want to spend.
About 10–15 min. Fits: Wants to get up; Suitable familiar outdoor route; Nearby open store confirmed; Small purchase accepted; Suitable daytime or evening.
Done: You returned with one chosen item.
C08 Open the curtains and sit for a minute
If daylight is available and privacy allows, put the phone down, open the curtains and sit facing the room for one minute.
About 1–2 min. Fits: Wants to get up; At home; Suitable daytime or evening; Daylight and privacy; Can be seated; Can be quiet.
Done: The curtains are open and you spent a minute off the screen.
C09 Get the first work item ready
Put the phone down and place the one book, tool or document you need for your first planned task on the desk. Open it to the page or file you need.
About 2–3 min. Fits: Wants to get up; Specific task to start; Work surface; Can be quiet.
Done: The first task is ready to start.
C10 Leave the bed for a seated activity
Move to a chair or your usual seated position. Put the phone down and spend three minutes looking at a physical book, magazine or printed page.
About 3 min. Fits: Wants to get up; At home; Physical book; Indoor movement suitable; Can be quiet.
Done: You spent three minutes out of your sleeping position.
